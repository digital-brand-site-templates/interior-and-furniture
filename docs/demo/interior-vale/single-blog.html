Five ways to make a small living room feel twice the size

12 September 2026 / By Alex Haltes

Most small living rooms are not short of space; they are short of sightlines. When the eye can travel from the door to the far wall without meeting the back of a sofa, a room reads as larger before anyone has measured it.

Start by pulling the largest piece away from the entrance. A sofa facing the door, rather than blocking it, instantly opens the room, and furniture on slim legs lets the floor run underneath.

A room should feel finished on the day you move in, and better ten years later.Alex Haltes, Founder

One large rug does more than three small ones. It should be big enough for the front feet of every seat to rest on it — that single decision ties the room together and stops it feeling bitty.

Keep the palette close. Walls, curtains and the largest upholstered piece in neighbouring tones let the edges of the room soften, while one darker accent — a chair, a lamp base, a frame — gives the eye somewhere to rest.

Finally, light the corners. A room lit only from the centre shrinks at night; two low lamps at opposite corners push the walls back out. If you do one thing this weekend, make it that.
